Many teams treat warm-up as the whole answer to deliverability. Turn on a tool, wait two weeks, start sending. Then they're surprised when replies dry up a month later.

## What warm-up actually does

Warm-up builds a short history of normal-looking activity on a new inbox. That matters, but it's only one input. Mailbox providers care far more about how *real* recipients react to your mail.

## What matters more

1. **List quality.** Verify every address and drop role accounts. High bounce rates do more damage than anything a warm-up tool can repair.
2. **Volume per inbox.** Keep each inbox to a modest daily volume and add inboxes to scale, rather than pushing more volume through each one.
3. **Relevance.** Emails that get replies teach providers your mail is wanted. Emails that get ignored or reported teach them the opposite.
4. **Plain formatting.** Few links, no tracking pixels on the first touch, no heavy HTML.

## A simple weekly check

Each week, look at bounce rate, reply rate and spam-placement tests side by side. If one moves, change one variable at a time until it recovers.
